1. Business Problem Statement:
Chronic disease patients—particularly those with hypertension and heart failure—face an elevated risk of acute hypertensive crises and heart failure exacerbations. These events frequently drive avoidable emergency admissions, worsen long-term health outcomes, and increase healthcare costs. Current monitoring methods are primarily reactive, identifying deterioration only after it occurs, and thus miss crucial opportunities for preventive intervention. There is a clear need for proactive, predictive monitoring to enable early, targeted care that can improve patient safety and reduce system strain.

2. Business Context:
- Industry: Healthcare (Providers, Telemedicine, Digital Health)
- Stakeholders: Patients with hypertension and/or heart failure; care teams (including nurses, physicians, and allied health professionals); healthcare administrators; IT departments; regulatory bodies (e.g., CMS, FDA); insurance companies and payers
- Systems: Electronic Health Record (EHR) platforms; patient wearable device ecosystems; telehealth scheduling and delivery solutions; care team notification and alerting systems; insurer data integration platforms; regulatory compliance and audit systems
- Additional Considerations: Integration of new technologies with legacy healthcare IT infrastructure may present significant challenges; robust data governance processes will be necessary to manage consent, data ownership, access, and interoperability.

3. Functional Requirements:
- Continuously ingest live vital sign data (e.g., blood pressure, heart rate) from diverse patient wearables and connected devices
- Seamless, bidirectional integration of real-time vital data with each patient’s Electronic Health Record (EHR) system
- Application of machine-learning models to predict 24–48 hour risk for hypertensive crisis or heart failure events, with clear risk stratification outputs
- Real-time notification and escalation workflows to care teams when patients exceed predefined risk thresholds, allowing for rapid response
- Automated scheduling and management of telehealth or in-person check-ins for at-risk patients, tailored to both provider availability and patient preferences
- Generation of comprehensive risk dashboards and longitudinal trend reports for both clinicians and patients, presenting actionable insights
- Full traceability and logging of all data flows, system actions, and user interventions to support governance and audit requirements

4. Non-Functional Requirements:
- Scalable ingestion and processing to support 10,000+ concurrent patients with data latency under 5 minutes per ingest event
- Robust compliance with HIPAA, GDPR, and all relevant local healthcare data privacy regulations, supported by clear data governance protocols (consent management, data lineage, retention policies)
- End-to-end encryption of data (at rest and in transit), coupled with granular and role-based access controls customizable to organizational and regulatory needs
- High system reliability and availability (≥99.9% uptime), with automated failover and disaster recovery capabilities to ensure business continuity during device or network outages
- Clinician user interfaces designed for efficiency and intuitive navigation; accessibility features designed for patients and clinicians with varying degrees of digital literacy and abilities
- Explainability and auditability of all AI-driven predictions, with user-friendly model rationale, risk factors, and audit trails to facilitate clinical trust and regulatory acceptance
- Detailed integration frameworks for interoperability with legacy EHRs and existing provider IT infrastructure, supported by interface standards (e.g., HL7, FHIR)

5. Emerging Need Justification:
Remote patient monitoring (RPM) adoption is accelerating due to advances in wearable sensor accuracy, machine learning capabilities, and increased comfort with telemedicine. Simultaneously, healthcare quality mandates are tightening, and both payers and regulators are shifting towards value-based care reimbursement models that reward the prevention of acute events. These trends increase the urgency and strategic value of scalable, predictive chronic disease management—enabling earlier detection, intervention, and improved outcomes. Effective solutions must not only leverage new technology but also navigate complex clinical, regulatory, and interoperability landscapes.

6. Expected Business Outcome:
- Achieve a ≥20% reduction in emergency cardiovascular admissions among enrolled patients within 12 months of deployment
- Improve patient engagement and adherence to care plans, as measured by system usage metrics and patient-reported experience
- Shorten time-to-intervention after risk alert by at least 50%, enabling timely clinical action
- Demonstrate measurable cost savings per patient for care providers and payers, supporting negotiations for value-based contracts
- Ensure regulatory and payer readiness through robust data governance, integration, and audit capabilities, paving the way for system scalability and broader adoption
